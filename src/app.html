Pros & cons of cramming the core

Works for you
  • Covers the concepts that recur across many questions, so effort compounds.
  • Fits a short runway — roughly 4 hours of drilling instead of 40.
  • Front-loads the memorization items (IR order, volatility, risk math, MFA factors) that are pure free points.
  • Builds the vocabulary you need to eliminate wrong answers on questions you have never seen.
Works against you
  • The 80/20 split is a study heuristic, not a published CompTIA statistic — treat it as a priority order, not a guarantee.
  • Performance-based questions pull from the long tail this list deliberately skips.
  • A 750/900 pass leaves little slack; core-only leaves you near the line, not above it.
  • Recognition beats recall here — the core teaches you to spot patterns, not to reason through novel scenarios.

Use the core to get to competent fast, then spend whatever time is left on full practice sets. Passing this month means core first, breadth second — not core only.

Exam simulation

The real SY0-701 exam is a maximum of 90 questions in 90 minutes, mixing multiple choice, multiple response, drag-and-drop, and performance-based items, and it is scored on a 100–900 scale with 750 to pass. This sim mirrors that: 4 performance-based items first, then 86 multiple-choice questions drawn in the real objective weighting — 11 from Domain 1, 20 from Domain 2, 16 from Domain 3, 25 from Domain 4, 18 from Domain 5.

One caution on scoring: 750/900 is not 75% correct. CompTIA weights items and PBQs can carry more weight, so no practice tool can convert a raw percentage into a true scaled score. This app reports raw percentage and uses a stricter 83% target so a pass here means real margin, not a coin flip.

Items leave the queue after box 4. Each set also mixes in unseen questions from the same objectives, so you have to recognise the concept in wording you have not already memorised.

Straight recall, no scenarios

Flashcards

Ports, acronyms and definitions are the cheapest marks on the exam: they need recall, not reasoning. Five minutes a day here converts directly into points.
